Research and innovation

Six themes organize ECIT's research. Descriptions are illustrative of the intended focus.

Semiconductor technology

MOSFET, CMOS, FinFET, and gate-all-around devices; leakage and power; lithography, etching, and deposition.

Explore theme

Advanced integrated circuits

Low-power design with clock gating and multi-voltage domains, plus formal verification and timing closure for complex ICs.

Explore theme

Wireless communications

Resilient links for 6G and challenging channels, with low-latency protocol design.

Explore theme

Signal processing

Estimation and adaptive filtering for noisy channels, with hardware-friendly implementations.

Explore theme

AI-assisted hardware

Machine learning to speed up chip design, verification, and test, and efficient accelerators for inference.

Explore theme

Emerging computing

RISC-V processors, chiplets and advanced packaging, and secure SoC architectures.

Explore theme

    Campus and community

    Labs, workshops, and shared studios where students and researchers build things together.

    Student projects

    From a 4-bit ripple carry adder and traffic light controller to a UART, an asynchronous FIFO with Gray-code pointers, an AXI4-Lite slave, and a pipelined RISC-V core. Advanced teams verify with UVM or formal methods.

    Engineering competitions

    Timed sprints on FSM state encoding, asynchronous FIFO depth, CDC handshakes, clock-gating power reduction, and maximum-frequency counter synthesis.

    Student projects

    From a link budget calculator and a digital modulation simulator to a software-defined radio receiver, an adaptive equalizer, and an antenna measurement setup. Illustrative examples.

    Engineering competitions

    Timed sprints on channel estimation, modulation classification, filter design, and HF link planning. Illustrative examples.

    About ECIT

    ECIT is a conceptual institute for electronics and communication engineering. This page is a design prototype: it makes no claims about accreditation, rankings, history, or research results.

    Academic vision

    Teach from fundamentals to systems, with studio and lab work in every year.

    Research priorities

    Semiconductors, wireless systems, signal processing, and AI-enabled hardware.

    Engineering focus

    Build, measure, and iterate on real hardware and real signals, using simulators and FPGA flows such as Icarus Verilog, GTKWave, and Verilator.
